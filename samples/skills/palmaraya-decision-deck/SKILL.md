# Build a deck that argues, not a deck that reports

A deck that reports forces the audience to draw its own conclusion. A deck that argues
states the conclusion first and puts the evidence underneath. Build the second kind.

## Every slide title is a statement

A title has to be a sentence that can be true or false. If the title is a noun phrase,
that is a topic, and it must be rewritten.

- "Extraction rate" becomes "Extraction rate has fallen seven months running, worth Rp 122 billion a year"
- "Mill losses" becomes "Sungai Rangkat is losing oil at three stations at once"
- "Harvest quality" becomes "Two divisions account for almost the entire rise in harvest losses"

This applies to every slide, including the agenda and closing slides. A title without a
verb is not finished.

## The closing slide asks for a decision

End with three decisions, each with a named owner and a date. Not "next steps", not
"recommendations".

Remove the words "monitor", "review again", "keep watching", and "will be studied
further" wherever they appear. Those are how a deck avoids asking for anything.

## Charts

Build every chart natively in PowerPoint. Never paste a chart in as an image. Images
cannot be edited, do not follow the theme colours, and break on a different screen.

Use columns, bars, lines, or stacked columns. Add data labels. Put one caption line under
the chart stating what it shows.

Do not build a chart for figures that are not in the source file. If the data is not
there, say which slide cannot be completed and why, rather than inventing a figure that
looks plausible.

## Speaker notes on every slide

Two things, in this order:

1. Two sentences on what to say while this slide is on screen.
2. The single question most likely to come from this slide, with a one-line answer.

The second part is the most useful line in the whole deck. Write it even when I forget
to ask for it.

## Density

Four bullets per slide maximum, one line per bullet. If it does not fit, the detail
belongs in the speaker notes, not on the slide. One idea per slide. If a slide carries
two findings, split it into two.

## Language

Write in plain, professional Indonesian. Use the company's standard terms: janjang, TBS,
rendemen, hancak, restan, brondolan, afdeling, blok, losses. Write numbers with a full
stop as the thousands separator and a comma as the decimal separator.

## Sequence for a new deck

Unless I give you a different structure, use this order:

1. The situation, stated neutrally, with figures
2. What has been believed about that situation
3. Why that belief does not survive contact with the data
4. What the data actually shows
5. The consequence, in rupiah
6. The cost of doing nothing
7. The action plan
8. The three decisions being asked for

## Finishing an existing deck

Keep the existing template, fonts, colours, and slide master. A deck that suddenly
switches to default Office styling halfway through looks pasted together.

Read the whole deck before changing anything. Count the slides and the empty
placeholders. When you report back, give the slide numbers you changed and what you did
to each one.

## Do not stop to ask

Finish the entire deck in one pass. Where you have to assume something, write the
assumption into the speaker notes of the slide concerned rather than stopping to ask.

## Before you finish, check

- Every title contains a verb and states a conclusion.
- The last slide carries three decisions, each with an owner and a date.
- The words "monitor" and "review again" appear nowhere.
- Every chart is native, has data labels, and has a caption.
- Every slide has speaker notes containing the likely question.
- No slide has more than four bullets.
- The original template is unchanged.
